SECTION A1: FOUNDATIONAL CONCEPTS 
A1.1 Securitisation Fundamentals 
What is Securitisation? 
Securitisation is the process of pooling various types of financial assets (loans, receivables, 
mortgages) and converting them into tradeable securities backed by the cash flows from those 
assets. The process involves transferring assets from an originator to a Special Purpose Vehicle 
(SPV), which issues securities (tranches) with varying risk-return profiles to investors. 
Key Structures: 
●​ Pass-Through Securities: Cash flows from underlying assets pass directly to investors 
proportionally 
●​ Pay-Through Securities (CMOs): Cash flows are restructured into sequential tranches 
with different maturities 
●​ Credit-Tranched Securities (CDOs/CLOs): Cash flows allocated by seniority-senior 
tranches absorb losses last, equity tranches absorb first

B. Pool-Level Statistics Required by Rating Agencies 
In addition to loan-level tapes, rating agencies require the following pool-level summary 
statistics. These are calculated using DAX measures in the Power BI model: 
Pool Composition Metrics: 
●​ Weighted Average Coupon (WAC): Interest rate weighted by current balance 
●​ Weighted Average Maturity (WAM): Remaining term weighted by current balance 
●​ Weighted Average Loan Age (WALA): Months on book weighted by current balance 
●​ Weighted Average LTV: Loan-to-value ratio weighted by current balance 
●​ Weighted Average CIBIL Score: Credit score weighted by current balance 
●​ Weighted Average DTI: Debt-to-income weighted by current balance

C. S&P Global - Specific Auto ABS Requirements 
S&P Global Ratings uses its LEVELS (Loan Evaluation and Estimate of Loss System) model for 
auto ABS. The model requires specific data inputs beyond the standard tape: 
LEVELS Model Inputs: 
●​ Base Case Loss Expectation: Derived from historical loss curves of the originator 
●​ Loss Timing Curve: Monthly distribution of expected losses 
●​ Recovery Lag: Average time from default to recovery (typically 3-6 months for auto) 
●​ Voluntary Prepayment Speed: Expressed as Absolute Prepayment Speed (ABS) or 
Conditional Prepayment Rate (CPR) 
●​ Recovery Rate by LTV Band: Different recovery assumptions for high-LTV vs low-LTV 
●​ Stress Multiples by Rating Level: AAA = 3.0x-4.0x base loss, AA = 2.5x-3.0x, A = 
2.0x-2.5x 
DAX for S&P Stress Scenarios: 
SP Stressed Loss AAA = 
VAR BaseLoss = \[Base Case Expected Loss\] 
VAR StressMultiple = 3.5 // AAA stress factor 
VAR StressedLoss = BaseLoss \* StressMultiple 
VAR CreditEnhancement = \[Senior Tranche CE Percentage\] 
RETURN 
IF(StressedLoss \> CreditEnhancement, \"INSUFFICIENT CE\", 
\"ADEQUATE 
CE\")

D. Moody's - Specific Auto ABS Requirements 
Moody's uses its Idealised Default Table and MILAN (Moody's Individual Loan Analysis) 
framework. Key additional data requirements: 
●​ Lognormal Loss Distribution Parameters: Mean and standard deviation of loss 
●​ Correlation Assumptions: Intra-pool asset correlation (typically 3-8% for auto) 
●​ Seasoning Adjustment: Performance credit for seasoned pools 
●​ Originator Assessment: Qualitative score of originator's underwriting standards 
●​ Servicer Quality Assessment: Score of servicer's collection capabilities 
E. Fitch Ratings - Specific Auto ABS Requirements 
Fitch uses a multi-step approach requiring: 
●​ Expected Cumulative Net Loss (CNL): Base case loss estimate 
●​ Loss Timing Distribution: Front-loaded vs back-loaded loss patterns 
●​ Rating Factor Multiples: Stress multiples applied to base case (AAAsf = 5.0x-6.0x for 
subprime, 3.0x-4.0x for prime) 
●​ Excess Spread Analysis: Monthly excess spread available to absorb losses 
●​ Liquidity Assessment: Ability of structure to meet payment obligations during stress 
F. Indian Rating Agencies - CRISIL and ICRA Requirements 
For Indian auto loan securitisations, CRISIL and ICRA require additional India-specific data: 
●​ RBI Priority Sector Classification: Whether loans qualify under PSL guidelines 
●​ GST Registration of Borrower: For commercial vehicle loans 
●​ Aadhaar Verification Status: KYC completeness 
●​ NACH Mandate Status: Auto-debit consent for EMI collection 
●​ Insurance Coverage: Comprehensive vs third-party vs uninsured 
●​ Hypothecation Registration: RC book endorsement status 
●​ Loan Restructuring Under RBI Moratorium: Covid moratorium status history

A2.9 Static Loss Data - Vintage/Static Pool Analysis 
Static pool analysis (also called vintage analysis or static loss analysis) is the gold standard 
methodology used by rating agencies to project expected losses for a securitised pool. Unlike 
dynamic analysis which looks at the current snapshot, static pool analysis tracks a fixed group of 
loans from origination to termination, regardless of when new loans are added or existing loans 
pay off. 
A. Static Pool Concept 
Definition: 
A static pool is a fixed set of loans originated during a specific time period (e.g., Q1 2022 
originations). The performance of this pool is tracked cumulatively over time from origination 
(Month 0) onwards. No loans are added to or removed from the static pool after the cutoff. This 
pure measurement of credit performance eliminates the distorting effects of pool growth, 
seasoning mix changes, and portfolio turnover. 
Why Rating Agencies Require Static Loss Data: 
●​ Eliminates denominator effect: New originations don't dilute delinquency rates 
●​ Pure credit performance: Measures only credit deterioration, not portfolio management 
●​ Vintage comparison: Enables direct comparison across origination cohorts 
●​ Loss curve projection: Allows extrapolation of partial curves to project ultimate losses 
●​ Originator assessment: Evaluates whether underwriting quality is stable, improving, or 
deteriorating over time 
 
Strictly Private and Confidential - Not for Circulation                                                                          Page number: 45

SECTION B1: SECURITISATION RISK ARENA - SIMULATION OVERVIEW 
B1.1 Business Rationale And Problem Statement 
Securitisation Risk Arena is a browser-based gamified simulation platform designed to train and 
assess data analysts in the specialised discipline of securitisation risk assessment through 
realistic, time-pressured, scenario-driven challenges using advanced DAX analytics. The player 
assumes the role of Lead Data Analyst at a fictional global bank - Meridian Structured Finance - 
managing a $50 billion portfolio of securitised assets across MBS, ABS, CLO, and CMBS 
structures. Each session simulates a full business day in the life of a securitisation data analyst: 
from receiving the morning portfolio health report to delivering risk dashboards to the Chief Risk 
Officer by end of day. 
The Training Gap This Platform Solves: 
Traditional securitisation analytics training relies on passive reading of rating agency 
methodologies and regulatory guidelines, occasional shadowing of senior analysts during deal 
closings or portfolio reviews, and learning by making errors on live reporting systems. The 
consequences of errors in securitisation analytics are particularly severe: an incorrect ECL 
computation can misstate the bank’s provision requirements by millions; a flawed waterfall 
calculation can misallocate cash flows between senior and subordinated tranches; a missed 
delinquency trend can delay early-warning triggers, leading to rating downgrades and investor 
losses. 
India’s securitisation market has grown significantly following the RBI’s revised securitisation 
framework (September 2021), with issuance volumes exceeding ₹1.8 lakh crore annually. Global 
structured finance markets process trillions in outstanding securities. Yet there is no standardised, 
scalable training platform for securitisation data analysts that combines DAX proficiency with 
domain expertise. Securitisation Risk Arena creates a zero-risk environment where learners 
experience every possible analytics scenario - from routine monthly reporting to crisis-mode 
stress testing - before encountering them in a live portfolio.

Target Audience: 
●​ Primary: Undergraduate and MBA students preparing for data analytics, structured

●​ Secondary: Working professionals in credit risk, portfolio analytics, and investor

●​ Tertiary: Bank HR and training teams seeking to assess structured finance analytics

B1.2 Simulation Architecture 
Core Components: 
●​ Portfolio Library: Pre-built datasets for 6 fictional securitisation pools (auto loan ABS, 
RMBS, CLO, credit card ABS, CMBS, student loan ABS) with 500+ loans per pool,

●​ Market Simulator: Monthly data generator that simulates delinquency transitions,

scenarios - benign, moderate stress, severe stress, and crisis. 
●​ DAX Challenge Engine: Presents the player with specific analytical tasks requiring DAX

computations, and time-intelligence measures. 
●​ Waterfall Engine: Simulated cash flow waterfall that allocates collections across tranches 
based on player-built DAX measures; errors in the waterfall formula cascade through 
tranche payments, revealing mistakes immediately. 
●​ Rating Agency Module: Requires the player to produce loan-level data tapes, pool

●​ ECL Engine: Embedded IFRS 9 computation module that evaluates player-built PD,

●​ Learning Layer: Every error triggers an explanation pop-up with the correct DAX

SECTION C1: THE 2008 GLOBAL FINANCIAL CRISIS - SECURITISATION 
MELTDOWN 
C1.1 Background 
The 2007–2009 Global Financial Crisis was, at its core, a securitisation crisis. US subprime 
mortgage-backed securities, which had been rated AAA by major rating agencies, experienced 
catastrophic default rates as the housing bubble burst. The crisis revealed fundamental flaws in 
securitisation analytics: models assumed perpetually rising house prices, correlation assumptions 
between mortgage defaults were grossly underestimated, and loan-level data quality was 
compromised by originate-to-distribute incentive misalignment. The collapse of Lehman 
Brothers, the bailout of AIG, and the freeze of global credit markets were direct consequences of 
securitisation risk being systematically underestimated.

SECTION C2: THE IL&FS CRISIS (2018) - INDIAN STRUCTURED FINANCE 
COLLAPSE 
C2.1 Background 
In September 2018, Infrastructure Leasing & Financial Services (IL&FS), one of India’s largest 
infrastructure finance companies, defaulted on its commercial paper and bond obligations, 
triggering a crisis across India’s structured finance and NBFC sectors. IL&FS group entities had 
issued securitised instruments backed by infrastructure project receivables and loan portfolios. 
The crisis exposed cascading risks: SPV cash flows were disrupted as underlying project 
payments stalled, investors in pass-through certificates (PTCs) faced payment delays, and the 
broader NBFC sector experienced a liquidity crunch as mutual funds and banks pulled back from 
structured finance exposure. 
C2.2 Data Analytics Implications 
Implication 1: Counterparty Concentration In Securitisation Pools 
Several securitisation pools had exposure to IL&FS group entities as borrowers or guarantors. 
The lesson: DAX measures for obligor concentration (top 10 borrower exposure, single-name 
concentration limits) are critical for securitisation analytics. Power BI dashboards must highlight 
counterparty risk at both loan-level and pool-level. 
Implication 2: Real-Time Collection Monitoring 
The IL&FS crisis demonstrated that monthly reporting cycles are insufficient for detecting cash 
flow disruptions in securitisation pools. DAX time-intelligence measures with daily or weekly 
granularity, combined with collection efficiency ratios (actual collections vs scheduled 
collections), provide the real-time monitoring that was absent. 
Implication 3: Rbi Regulatory Response 
Post-IL&FS, the RBI revised its securitisation framework (September 2021), introducing stricter 
Minimum Holding Period and Minimum Retention Requirements. The revised framework 
requires originators to retain a minimum 10% of each tranche issued, ensuring skin-in-the-game. 
DAX measures must track compliance with these requirements in investor reporting dashboards. 
C2.3 Securitisation Risk Arena Simulation Scenario

assess the ECL impact under an immediate default assumption, check whether the pool’s credit 
enhancement is sufficient to absorb the concentrated loss, and prepare a risk assessment note for 
the credit committee.

SECTION C3: RBI REVISED SECURITISATION FRAMEWORK (2021) 
C3.1 Background 
The Reserve Bank of India issued its revised framework for securitisation of standard assets in 
September 2021 (RBI/2021-22/85 DOR.STR.REC.51/21.04.177/2021-22), replacing the 2006

repayment frequency, simplified securitisation structures with only two categories (securitisation 
and direct assignment), and enhanced disclosure requirements for originators and investors.

Straight-Through Data Processing:

C4.2 Lessons For Intern Data Analysts

the single most important technical skill. 
●​ Data Quality is Non-Negotiable: The value of analytics depends entirely on data quality. 
Loan-level data must be complete, consistent, and reconcilable to source systems. DAX 
IFERROR and validation measures are essential. 
●​ Business Context Elevates Technical Work: A DAX measure that computes a 
delinquency rate is technical; understanding why that rate matters for tranche investors, 
rating agencies, and regulators transforms the analyst from a technician to a business 
advisor. 
●​ Documentation and Audit Trail: Every DAX measure must be documented in a measure 
dictionary with its business purpose, formula, and expected behaviour. This is 
non-negotiable in regulated financial services.
